# Wave 1 counter-evidence

Wave 1 supplies positive problem and usefulness signals, but it also places clear limits on product claims.

| Counter-evidence | Direct response evidence | Constraint on PULSE claims |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing tools may be sufficient | R05 and R07 selected existing tools as sufficient. All current-tool ratings were 3 or 4; mean 3.33/5. | PULSE cannot claim a universally unmet tooling gap. It must show incremental value against existing CI, source hosts, dashboards, bots, scripts, and package tools. |
| Automation trust is uneven | R04 and R07 would not rely on automated conclusions; five selected lack of trust as a barrier. | A usefulness rating does not establish reliance. PULSE must retain evidence qualification and avoid autonomous conclusions. |
| False positives may block adoption | Four selected false positives as a barrier; five reported irrelevant alerts. | Candidate volume cannot be treated as value. Artifact grouping, origin, explanation, and review quality remain required. |
| Human review can erase value | Three selected too much human review; only two selected human review as a trust prerequisite. | Review cannot be assumed desirable at any volume. Burden and escalation need measurement before workflow expansion. |
| Integration effort is material | Five selected integration effort as a barrier. | A standalone evidence view may fail even if analytically useful. No integration benefit or cost is established. |
| Security and private data constrain evidence breadth | Four selected security/privacy concerns; two selected missing private/internal data. | Broader collection requires authorization, minimization, retention, and explicit unavailable states. Public-only results may remain incomplete. |
| Some results may not affect decisions | R06 and R07 selected that results would not affect decisions. | The survey does not prove PULSE changes migration or remediation choices. |
| Problem frequency may be low | R07 selected that the problem does not occur frequently enough. | No claim about recurring demand, time savings, ROI, or operational priority is supported. |
| Value ratings are dispersed | One usefulness rating was 2, three were 3, one was 4, and four were 5. | Interest is supported but not uniform; the mean 3.89/5 is not demand or adoption intent. |
| Follow-up commitment is limited | One Yes, four Maybe, four No. | Wave 1 cannot be treated as stakeholder signoff or a committed design partnership. |

The strongest direct counterexample to a universal PULSE thesis is that current tools are at least moderately supportive for all respondents and explicitly sufficient for two. The strongest design constraints are trust and integration, each selected by 5/9. These findings favor a narrow evidence-support role and challenge broader automation claims.
