# Wave 1 thematic analysis

The codebook was applied to the frozen anonymized matrix at respondent grain. Counts show respondents with at least one coded response for the theme; multi-select answers can support several themes. **Direct response evidence** below reports selections or paraphrased content. **Analyst interpretation** is a separate inference and is not respondent testimony. Confidence applies only to this convenience sample of n = 9.

| Theme | Direct response evidence and respondent IDs | Counter-evidence | Analyst interpretation | Confidence and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Identifying affected repositories | 7 respondents: R01, R02, R04, R05, R06, R07, R08. Five chose it as most useful; three reported it as manual effort and three as an encountered problem. | R03 and R09 did not select the theme. | Repository identification is a meaningful investigation task for much of Wave 1. | High within sample; actual time or decision effect unmeasured. |
| B. Actual/historical dependency versions | 8 respondents: R02–R09 selected version-use effort, historical-state difficulty/trust, or installed-version difficulty. Historical difficulty mean was 3.22/5. | R01 supplied no direct historical-state signal; only one chose reconstruction as the single most useful feature. | Evidence depth aligns with a real but not universally top-ranked need. | Moderate-high; declarations versus resolved versions were not experimentally tested. |
| C. Evidence fragmentation | 7/9 used at least two source types; R02 and R08 explicitly selected evidence spread across systems. | R03 and R07 selected only issues; explicit fragmentation pain was only 2/9. | Multi-source work is common in the sample, while “fragmentation” as a named pain is narrower. | Moderate; source use does not prove harmful fragmentation. |
| D. Breaking-change investigation | R01, R02, R04 selected breaking-change understanding as manual effort; compatibility/deprecation also appeared as triggers. | Six did not select this manual-effort item. | Breaking-change interpretation matters for a subset rather than defining every workflow. | Moderate-low. |
| E. Causal uncertainty | All 9 reported insufficient causal evidence at least rarely; 6/9 said sometimes. R02, R03, R04, R07, R08 selected unclear upgrade causality as a problem. | Three selected rarely; one assumes unrelated until proven otherwise. | Explicit causal limits and qualified language match a recurring uncertainty. | High within sample; no incident outcomes were audited. |
| F. Remediation/migration completion | All 9 supplied completion signals; R02, R04, R06, R08 selected completion as manual effort, and R02/R05 selected completion difficulty. | Criteria vary widely from version update to tests, runtime, monitoring, deployment, approval, and documentation. | Completion is real but cannot be represented by one universal artifact. | High for heterogeneity; low for a single rule. |
| G. Manual effort | R01, R02, R08, R09 selected too much manual investigation; R06 described a manual, time-consuming process. | Four did not explicitly report excess manual effort; no time estimate was collected. | Manual work is present, but labor savings are unquantified. | Moderate. |
| H. False positives / alert fatigue | R02, R04, R06, R07, R08 selected irrelevant alerts and/or false positives; false positives were a barrier for 4/9. | Four supplied no alert-fatigue signal. | False-positive control is a meaningful adoption constraint. | Moderate-high; PULSE-specific false-positive tolerance was not tested. |
| I. Trust in automated findings | R01, R02, R04, R07, R08, R09 expressed a trust barrier or reluctance to rely on automation. All respondents selected evidence prerequisites. | Four did not select lack of trust as a barrier; usefulness remained positive overall. | Automation must remain explainable and evidence-qualified. | High for trust conditions, lower for actual reliance. |
| J. Provenance / traceability | All 9 rated traceability at least 3/5; 7/9 rated 4 or 5. R01, R02, R08 requested original links. | R03 and R06 rated 3; only 3/9 explicitly selected source links as a trust input. | PULSE provenance is well aligned with stated preferences. | High within sample; no PULSE trace was usability-tested. |
| K. Missing evidence / uncertainty handling | All 9 encountered insufficient evidence at least rarely and selected an output preference. R02, R06, R08 requested a clear missing-evidence indicator. | Output preferences split across likely answer with uncertainty, confidence score, explicit UNKNOWN, and non-reliance. | Missingness must remain explicit, but presentation requires validation. | High for need to handle uncertainty; mixed for output design. |
| L. Centralized evidence | R02/R08 reported evidence spread; R04/R09 chose centralized evidence as most useful; R04/R08 described consolidation as useful. | Five did not identify centralization directly. | A consolidated evidence view has supported interest, not universal demand. | Moderate. |
| M. CI/test/runtime evidence | R01, R02, R04, R06, R08, R09 selected CI, tests, or runtime logs as sources/trust inputs. CI/test evidence was required by 5/9. | Package documentation was the most-confidence source for 3/9; context varies. | CI/test/runtime evidence materially affects confidence for a majority of the sample. | High within sample; eligibility and environment semantics remain undefined. |
| N. Workflow integration | R01, R02, R04, R07, R08 selected integration effort as a barrier. | Four did not select it; no integration architecture was evaluated. | Integration cost could erase value and deserves a separate requirement candidate. | Moderate-high. |
| O. Security/privacy concerns | R02, R03, R06, R08 selected security/privacy as a barrier. | Five did not select it. | Security review is material and cannot be deferred when private sources are considered. | Moderate. |
| P. Internal/private evidence | R02 and R08 selected missing internal/private data. | Seven did not; no private-source access policy was tested. | Public evidence may be insufficient for some workflows, but breadth is unknown. | Low-moderate. |
| Q. Existing tools sufficient | R05 and R07 selected existing-tool sufficiency; current-tool support was 3 or 4 for every respondent. | Seven still reported problems or positive usefulness. | Current tools are not uniformly inadequate; PULSE must demonstrate an incremental gap. | High for counter-evidence, mixed overall. |
| R. Frequency / importance | Insufficient causal evidence was “sometimes” for R03–R08 and “rarely” for R01, R02, R09. R07 said the problem may not occur often enough. | The survey did not measure upgrade frequency, hours, delay, or impact. | Importance has a positive signal, but occurrence and economic materiality remain unresolved. | Low. |
| S. Human review burden | R01, R02, R08 selected excessive review as a barrier; R02/R08 also requested human review before trust. | Six did not select review burden; only two selected review as a trust prerequisite. | Human review is both a safeguard and a possible adoption cost. | Moderate-low. |
| T. Stakeholder authority | All 9 named an owner, split across technical lead, shared decision, individual engineer, engineering manager (2 each), and security team (1). | No category exceeds 2/9. | Investigator, reviewer, and final owner must remain separate and configurable. | High for heterogeneity; organizational context is absent. |

No theme count is a population prevalence estimate. Respondents without direct upgrade experience remain included but are not treated as equal evidence for observed workflow details.
