# Stakeholder validation Wave 2 synthesis

## 1. Wave scope

Wave 2 freezes a cumulative n = 14 export containing the original nine responses plus five additions, R10–R14. Wave 1 remains unchanged.

## 2. Sample

Ten respondents report direct upgrade experience. Roles span software, full-stack, backend, data/AI, and security/supply-chain engineering. Seven report multi-repository scope; four report one repository and three are unsure.

## 3. Strongest supported findings

Historical/version evidence, operational evidence, provenance, explanation, explicit missingness, and qualified automation receive repeated support. Traceability remains high at mean 4.07/5.

## 4. Mixed findings

Insufficient-evidence presentation, current-tool adequacy, centralization, human review, and automation remain mixed. Affected-repository identification remains the cumulative plurality but receives no top selection among additions.

## 5. Contradictions

One stable final decision owner remains contradicted. Three respondents say tools may be sufficient, four would not rely on automation, two report possible decision non-impact, and two report low frequency.

## 6. Unanswered questions

Actual frequency, time, delay, cost, acceptable false-positive volume, PULSE comprehension, integration path, private-source authorization, installed-version source, recovery definition, and decision impact remain unresolved.

## 7. Requirements implications

Strengthen scope authority, provenance, exact missing prerequisites, historical/version and eligible operational evidence, completion criteria, security/privacy, and explainable triage. Reword singular role and universal-review assumptions. Keep recovery deferred and workflow integration as a candidate.

## 8. Persona implications

The engineer-investigator remains partially confirmed. Technical lead is a plurality owner, not a stable majority. Investigator, reviewer, repository owner, platform team, manager, security owner, and final decision owner remain separate.

## 9. Trust and uncertainty implications

Trust concern strengthens to 9/14. Explicit UNKNOWN rises to 3/14 and non-reliance to 4/14. `UNKNOWN != NO`; confidence aids cannot alter the finding state.

## 10. Value signal

Wave 2 preserves **SUPPORTED INTEREST**. Usefulness mean is 3.86/5, without observed use, adoption, ROI, or decision effect.

## 11. Phase 6 recommendation

The sequenced evidence-depth-first strategy remains `PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED`. Evidence integrity is supported; automation, review, integration, and frequency remain constrained.

## 12. Phase 7 result

`GO_BOUNDED` remains `PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED`. The method aligns with preferences but has not been used in a stakeholder decision.

## 13. G8 assessment

G8 remains `PARTIALLY_VALIDATED`. Recurrence and sample breadth improve, but task-based acceptance, owner authority, and decision impact are absent.

## 14. What future evidence should test

Run bounded cases with experienced upgrade owners, technical leads, repository owners, platform teams, managers, and security reviewers. Test evidence-chain comprehension, repository selection, review burden, integration, private-source policy, completion/recovery, and changed action.

## 15. What should not change yet

Do not weaken UNKNOWN or bounded-NO rules, claim validated demand or ROI, automate remediation, broaden private collection, collapse roles, promote experimental evidence-depth metrics, rewrite frozen Wave 1, or begin Phase 8.
