# Phase 2 findings as business-analysis inputs

This is an investigation map, not a requirements catalogue. “Observed” refers to the frozen Phase 2 validation only. Potential implications are hypotheses to test with stakeholders, not claims of pain or product value. See [Phase 2 findings](../phase-2/phase-2-findings.md), [historical reconstruction](../phase-2/historical-dependency-reconstruction.md), [detector evaluation](../phase-2/detector-evaluation.md), and [evidence sufficiency](../phase-2/evidence-sufficiency.md).

| ID | Phase 2 finding and observed evidence | Potential stakeholder/workflow implication | BA question to investigate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BA-01 | Historical target-package declaration evidence was established for **2/8** frozen validation repositories; reconstruction was path-bounded. This is evidence coverage, **not adoption**. | A list of declared repositories may be too weak or incomplete for a release-time decision. | What evidence is needed before considering a repository exposed to the target dependency release? |
| BA-02 | PULSE did not establish target-version installation or ordinary runtime/CI resolution. | “Uses the dependency” may mean declaration, resolved installation, compatibility, or different states to different users. | Which of those states must be known before the stakeholder acts, and what can remain UNKNOWN? |
| BA-03 | Downstream releases were not collected, so no recovery-eligible denominator or observed recovery measure was established. | “Recovered” may not mean shipping a new release in every workflow. | What does recovery mean operationally, and what timestamp or artifact would demonstrate it? |
| BA-04 | Two emitted candidates came from one Dependabot PR; both were rejected, with no accepted lifecycle events and no measurable recall. | Candidate review might cost more than it helps if signals are weak. | Which signals merit analyst review, and what review burden is acceptable? |
| BA-05 | Reports preserved UNKNOWN for unsupported findings, leaving many repository findings unresolved. | Honest uncertainty may help triage or may leave the decision blocked. | When is UNKNOWN useful, and which missing evidence prevents action? |
| BA-06 | A current dependency declaration did not establish historical release-time use. | Teams may need a point-in-time state aligned to their actual decision. | At what date/ref must dependency state be known? |
| BA-07 | Core bounded collection and report integration worked for the frozen cases, yet did not produce sufficient downstream event evidence. | More reliable collection alone may not solve the user's information problem. | Which additional source, case, or workflow step would change the decision? |
| BA-08 | The frozen cohort covered pytest and urllib3 with eight repositories selected under a constrained search method; it was not a representative sample of all downstreams. | Usefulness may vary by ecosystem, repository type, and ownership. | Which repository population does the stakeholder actually own or investigate? |
| BA-09 | Phase 1 HTTPX validation used connector-assisted snapshots; Phase 2 live API validation had explicit collection boundaries and provenance. | Users may need to distinguish independently collected, assisted, and fixture evidence when trusting a report. | What provenance and collection-completeness disclosure is needed for a decision or audit? |

The 2/8 historical result must never be read as a 25% adoption rate. No Phase 2 observation establishes that a stakeholder experiences these issues, wants PULSE, or would accept its outputs. [The discovery plan](discovery-plan.md) tests those propositions.
