# Stakeholder hypothesis register

All groups below are **hypotheses**, inferred from PULSE's repository-bounded use case. There is no recorded stakeholder interview or endorsement. Influence and impact are provisional relative assessments for discovery prioritization, not organizational facts. Roles can overlap; recruitment should test who actually owns the decision.

| Group | Hypothesized role and interest | Possible decision | Information they may require | Influence / impact (unverified) | Supporting project evidence | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Platform or dependency-management engineer | Coordinates dependency upgrades across owned repositories; may need a review queue. | Which repositories need investigation after a significant upstream release? | Point-in-time dependency state, issue/PR signals, provenance, missing evidence. | High / high | PULSE's declared-repository boundary and Phase 2 historical evidence gap suggest this workflow, but no person confirmed it. | hypothesis |
| Developer-productivity engineer | Maintains upgrade tooling and may want to reduce repetitive investigation. | Where should tooling or analyst attention go? | Existing-tool coverage, false-positive burden, reproducible reports. | Medium / medium | Phase 2 emitted two rejected bot-PR candidates; relevance to this role is untested. | hypothesis |
| Application-team technical lead | Owns one or more consuming services and migration decisions. | Does this release require action in our service, and when is work complete? | Verified version state, compatibility evidence, remediation and recovery criteria. | Medium / high | Recovery and installed-version evidence were absent in Phase 2; no actual team workflow was observed. | hypothesis |
| Engineering manager | May allocate migration effort and request status. | Which upgrade investigations need staffing or escalation? | Decision summary, uncertainty, effort and risk context. | High / medium | PULSE reports could support this; no management demand is documented. | hypothesis |
| Open-source maintainer | May seek evidence of downstream friction after releasing a library. | Which downstream reports merit response or compatibility guidance? | Attributable public evidence, repository relevance, chronology without causal overclaim. | Medium / medium | Original release-forensics thesis includes public downstream signals; no maintainer validation exists. | hypothesis |
| Software supply-chain team | May oversee dependency exposure or policy evidence. | Which owned repositories have unresolved dependency-state uncertainty? | Provenance, collection completeness, version evidence, auditability. | Medium / medium | PULSE tracks provenance and UNKNOWN; this is not evidence of a supply-chain use case. | hypothesis |

Prioritize discovery with people who personally performed or directed a recent dependency-release investigation. The [primary-user hypothesis](primary-user-hypothesis.md) selects a starting segment for testing, not an exclusive target or validated persona. Promote a group beyond `hypothesis` only with cited genuine evidence and a recorded validation decision.

Milestone 1A [public PR observations](secondary-research/github-workflow-analysis.md) show that some repository reviewers approve, comment on, or command dependency-update PRs. They do not establish those reviewers' organizational role, preferences, or a cross-repository decision remit. The stakeholder statuses above therefore remain `hypothesis` pending primary research.
