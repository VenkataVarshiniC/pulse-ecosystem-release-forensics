# Phase 6 executive summary

## 1. Problem

PULSE can reliably collect, govern, reproduce, and present a bounded public evidence record, but the available evidence often does not satisfy the prerequisites for a lifecycle conclusion. The next decision is where to invest to increase answerability without weakening the rules that keep missing evidence from becoming a false negative or unsupported claim.

## 2. What PULSE demonstrated

For two published validation investigations covering eight declared repositories, PULSE demonstrated bounded authenticated collection, immutable provenance, exact-ref observations, candidate/review separation, deterministic replay, governed PostgreSQL/dbt publication, a FastAPI and React investigation workflow, and Power BI decision reporting. Scope isolation, zero-denominator behavior, inherited review semantics, and drill-through were technically validated.

This is a bounded technical result. It is not evidence of production scalability, market demand, ecosystem coverage, or stakeholder value.

## 3. What the evidence shows

- Core planned collection completed for 8/8 members.
- The governed population contains 40 lifecycle findings: 38 `UNKNOWN`, 2 bounded `NO_QUALIFYING_EVIDENCE_FOUND`, and 0 supported positive findings.
- Exact-ref target declarations exist for 2/8 members, while paired pre-release historical context exists for 1/8.
- Historical context contains 1 `PAIRED`, 6 `AFTER_ONLY`, and 9 `UNRESOLVED` records.
- Two detector candidates share one copied-upstream artifact; both have inherited `REJECT` dispositions. They are not independent incidents, and rejection is not evidence that migration friction was absent.
- Observed adoption and recovery remain unavailable because qualifying inputs and eligible denominators are absent.

The evidence supports a specific diagnosis: planned-source collection reliability is stronger than question-specific analytical sufficiency. It does not show that all public evidence is inadequate or that more evidence necessarily creates value.

## 4. What remains unresolved

The repository does not establish a validated decision owner, required lifecycle question set, acceptable source threshold, reviewer authority, workflow value, demand, ROI, or completion definition. It also does not show that deeper history, resolved-version sources, or recovery evidence will be obtainable. `G8 = NOT_YET_VALIDATED`.

## 5. Decision

PULSE should prioritize evidence depth before cohort breadth or further workflow expansion. That priority must be implemented as a gated sequence rather than an open-ended source or feature program.

## 6. Recommendation

Select a **sequenced, evidence-depth-first strategy**:

1. Freeze a bounded evidence-depth experiment around an explicit lifecycle question and population.
2. Test stronger historical reconstruction, source-availability classification, question-specific prerequisites/reasons, and finding-support linkage.
3. Compare results side by side with the current method without modifying frozen or published evidence.
4. Progress to a source trial, stakeholder/workflow test, or selective cohort expansion only when the corresponding gate is satisfied.
5. Stop, narrow, or pivot if required evidence is inaccessible or the method does not improve bounded answerability.

## 7. Roadmap

- **NOW:** Specify and validate the evidence-depth experiment, historical method, source/reason taxonomy, replay comparison, and stop criteria.
- **NEXT:** Run one authorized question-specific source trial, validate positive support linkage when eligible evidence exists, and perform stakeholder reconciliation when authorized.
- **LATER:** Refine an observed investigator/reviewer workflow, test method transfer on an outcome-blind additional cohort, and reassess product/production investment.

The roadmap has no invented dates. Broad collection, workflow writes, and production expansion remain deferred.

## 8. Key risks

The required evidence may be unavailable; new source or history logic may overfit two cases; reason and prerequisite contracts may drift across data, API, UI, and BI; more artifacts may increase review noise; broader cohorts may amplify UNKNOWN; and a technically stronger result may still lack a stakeholder-owned decision. Existing tools and manual investigation remain credible substitutes.

## 9. Stakeholder-validation caveat

Phase 6 provides a defensible technical and analytical recommendation, not stakeholder approval. It claims no validated demand, utility, ROI, adoption, compatibility, recovery, or ecosystem impact. The classification is **PASS_WITH_STAKEHOLDER_HOLD**, and G8 remains `NOT_YET_VALIDATED`.
